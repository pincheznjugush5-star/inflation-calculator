How the maths works

Converting between years

Value in year 2 equals the amount in year 1 multiplied by the ratio of their index values: amount × (index2 / index1). This is the same method the Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes.

Pre-1913 figures

The official CPI begins in 1913. Earlier years use historical estimates rescaled to the modern index base, so the two series join without a step. Treat them as close approximations, not precise measurements.

Projections

Future values compound your chosen rate annually from the last measured index value. Inflation is volatile, so a single rate is a simplifying assumption. Test a few rates to see the range of outcomes.

Data: US Bureau of Labor Statistics (CPI-U, 1913–present) and the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis (historical estimates, 1850–1912). Figures before 1913 are estimates. Not financial advice.
